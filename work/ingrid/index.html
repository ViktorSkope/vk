The challenge

We were asked to build software on top of proprietary hardware for autonomous machinery.

The existing solution was highly technical and demanded programming expertise. That cost field technicians development time and left error handling far from ideal.

  • Understanding a problem meant reading the full log
  • Errors arrived as codes to memorize or look up on a cheat sheet
  • Some errors were only signal issues, with no real situation behind them

Our approach

We worked directly with a researcher and two field technicians to see how they were onboarded and how they monitored machines day to day.

  1. Map the journeys

    A four-week discovery mapped the user journeys, to understand the team's challenges and how they affected the company.

  2. Reframe the problem

    Technicians had seconds to choose between shutting down or fixing the issue later. So instead of only improving onboarding, we asked: what if they approached the product through reaction rather than action?

  3. Test against real decisions

    We tested prototypes with the technical team, checking each flow against the decisions they make in real work.

  4. Establish the brand

    A two-week visual identity sprint produced a styleguide and UI kit, so the product could be shown at conferences and expositions.

The solution

Warnings now tell technicians what is happening and give them time to respond, rather than asking them to decode an error.

  • Emergency and high-alert warnings start a countdown to shutoff, and dismissing the warning keeps the machine operating
  • Major errors flagged for auto-shutdown can be postponed until the technician knows more, or left to the automatic response
  • Simple recurring errors stay on screen without interrupting the work

The results

Onboarding went from an hour-long code workshop to an eight-minute prototype covering every warning case.

Controlled studies showed 100% adoption within two sessions at most, and usually after one. The MVP also saves the company 1.5 million USD a year in licensing fees for other training and monitoring software.
