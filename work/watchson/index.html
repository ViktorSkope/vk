The challenge

Developers were asked to fill in a form every six months with their skills, areas of focus and project mood. They rarely did.

That left managers without the information they needed to allocate people to agency projects. It was a tool the team had always wanted to build, but lacked the headcount for.

Our approach

We started by playing with the people who would use it, running game sessions with developers, mostly card games, and brainstorming ideas with them.

  1. Listen to developers

    Interviews and game sessions showed that a few developers had already turned many parts of their work and life into games. That gave us the insight we needed.

  2. Find the mechanic

    Football manager games build player cards from chemistry, skill level and motivation before a match. Here, a match is a client's project, and each person gets a fit score for it.

  3. Bring team leads in as peers

    Team leaders joined the project as peers, which set clear expectations. Success depended on their ability to manage, update and help developers find their matching skills.

  4. Explore the world around it

    We tried ideas that suited the people using the platform: achievements, avatars, improvements and a flock of ducks. Too many ducks.

The solution

The skills form became a team-building game, giving managers a clear view of their workforce and developers a reason to keep their skills current.

  • A welcome email invites each developer to create an account, then log, track or improve their skills
  • Project simulations rank people by level and fit, and compare the team's skills with what the project needs
  • Managers see hours, projects, skills and each person's mood in one place, so allocation stops being guesswork

The results

Before Watchson, only 2–3 of 75 developers kept their skills profiles updated through forms.

In a controlled time study, 95% created profiles and kept using the platform, boosting and testing skills with their team leads without being asked.
